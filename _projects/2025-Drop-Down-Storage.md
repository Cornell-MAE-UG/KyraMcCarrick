As part of my MAE 2250 (Introduction to Mechanical Design) course, my team ("4Store") was tasked with designing an aesthetically pleasing, highly functional storage solution for small apartments and dorms. We focused on the universal problem of unoptimized vertical space, specifically in closets, where the back and top areas are often completely inaccessible.

### Brainstorming & Ideation
We initially brainstormed 15 different concepts—ranging from lecture desk extenders to "lazy susans" for the ceiling. After evaluating them based on feasibility and impact, we pursued a drop-down closet shelf. The core concept was a shelf plate that could slide forward on rails and drop down, allowing easy access to items stored in the back of the closet.

### Prototyping Challenges & Iteration
Our first physical prototype was intended to be laser-cut from marine-grade plywood. However, we quickly discovered at the Rapid Prototyping Lab (RPL) that this specific wood was too dense for the laser cutters. We had to pivot to manually cutting the pieces using a bandsaw, which cost us significant time and precision. 

For our next iteration, we switched to 1/8" and 1/4" black acrylic to ensure smooth, precise laser cutting. We also completely overhauled the collapsing mechanism. Our early prototype used strings, which allowed the shelf to hang with too much slack and instability. We replaced this with a rigid system using T-shaped alignment pins slotted into custom 3D-printed fasteners. This allowed the plates to stack smoothly and securely without bulky hinges taking up space.

### Final Product Design
The final product successfully stores up to 5 pounds on the collapsible shelf and 10-15 pounds on the base plate. Using friction hinges and a magnetic locking backplate, it can be smoothly slid out and deployed in under 10 seconds.
