I grew up riding bikes, and love the freedom they give. When in Austin for a summer internship, I needed a way to get around the city, so I decided to get a project bike I could work on and ride. After a close inspection in person, I bought a 1980s Sanwa Technique off of Facebook Marketplace.

I also started volunteering with The Yellow Bike Project in Austin, where if you get enough volunteer hours, you can restore a bike with their shop and parts for free. A bill of materials can be found at the bottom of this page with all used and new parts I either bought or scavenged for free.

### Rims, Tires, and Pedals

<figure style="float: left; width: 33%; min-width: 200px; margin: 0 20px 15px 0; text-align: center;">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/Pedals.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Rockbros Pedals" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 6px; box-shadow: 2px 2px 6px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.9em; color: #666; margin-top: 8px;"><em>New Rockbros flat pedals</em></figcaption>
</figure>

After an initial cleaning and assessment of the bike, I decided to start with the wheels. I bought two new tubes, and found two used Bontrager tires. I had a little bit of space to increase the tire width from 25 mm to 32 mm. Though the rims were in true, I noticed the back axle was bent, and that both rims were quite hefty. I decided to replace them both and upgrade from a six-speed to an eight-speed rear cassette.

I found two different used 700c rims in the back and trued them. I then found an eight-speed cassette to fit on the back wheel. It took a lot of searching and trial and error to find a cassette combo that worked.

Another initial fix were the pedals. The old pedals were very small, and only had tread on one side, which made pedaling difficult. I found black Rockbros flat pedals that were double-sided, and replaced the old ones.

### Crankset Side Note

The original Biopace crankset is actually an oval, not circular, which changes the mechanical advantage of the gear depending on where you are in the stroke, smoothing out the pedaling motion. The effective radius is smaller during the power stroke (where the cranks are horizontal), and larger during the "dead zone" (where the cranks are vertical).

This allows you to build momentum during the power stroke where you have a lower gear and carry it through the dead zone, reducing the strain on your knees during the change of direction and smoothing out the stroke.

This is the opposite of some other crankset designs, which give you the lower gear during the dead spot, and the higher gear during the power stroke. This other idea was sound in theory, as it gives you a higher gear in the spot where you can generate more power, but was ineffective in practice and led to heightened knee pain.

### Drivetrain Tuning

<figure style="float: right; width: 30%; min-width: 250px; margin: 0 0 15px 20px; text-align: center;">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/Limiting_Screw.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Derailleur Limiting Screws" style="width: 80%; border-radius: 6px; box-shadow: 2px 2px 6px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
  <figcaption style="font-size: 0.9em; color: #666; margin-top: 8px;"><em>High Limit Screw Loosened All the Way</em></figcaption>
</figure>

After cleaning the chain in a coffee can and replacing the shifting cables and shifting cable housing, I began slowly tuning the friction shifters. The first thing I noticed was that the chain was rubbing against the derailleur in the highest gears. I removed a link to pull the bottom part of the derailleur down, which created some clearance between the chain and the derailleur.

As this bike was originally a 6-speed, the derailleur is not entirely compatible with the new cassette, and so the highest gear is still a little bit sticky. You can see that the limiting screw is loosened all the way, which isn't ideal. In the future, I might upgrade the derailleur.

### Brakes and Handlebars

I wanted to change to black handlebar wrap and get new cabling for the brakes, so I began with removing the original white handlebar wrap. The white wrap gets dirty too fast, and was starting to degrade due to its age. The completion of this restoration is still in progress.
