More recently, I have applied these design and assembly skills to the more functional material of wood. I have been creating projects that not only serve an aesthetic purpose, but that I also use in my day to day life. I made a wooden table using machines, as well as a small bookshelf using handtools and dovetail joinery. 

![Shaded rendering of earlier version]({{ "/assets/images/ShelfFull.jpg" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-r style="width: 200px"}
![Shaded rendering of earlier version]({{ "/assets/images/ShelfEmpty.jpg" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-r style="width: 200px"}

I took a woodworking class over the summer, and learned technical skills as well as safety for basic woodshop machines. Over the course of a week, I built a table from scratch, following given design specs. From long planks of rough cherry wood, we marked off usable sections, and cut workable pieces using the chop saw. We then moved to the jointer and planer, where we turned the warped wood into planks with right angles and parallel faces.

We used mortise and tenon joinery for the legs, which had a very small margin of error for the perfect fit required for strong glue adhesion. This involved the making a jig table saw and using the mortise machine. You can always cut away more wood, but once its gone its very hard to put it back. I learned to be precise and careful with my cuts, and as the saying goes, "measure twice cut once."

After the table was assembled, I then researched finishes, and decided on a mixture of Tung Oil and Spar Varnish. The cherry wood darkened beatifully, and I ended up with a lovely bedside table that is in my room at home.
